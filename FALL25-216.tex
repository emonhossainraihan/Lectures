\item \textbf{Solution}

\[
\int_C \frac{z}{z^2+4}\,dz,\quad |z|=3
\]

The singularities are at $z=\pm 2i$, both inside the contour.

\[
\frac{z}{z^2+4}
=\frac12\left(\frac{1}{z-2i}+\frac{1}{z+2i}\right)
\]

Sum of residues $=1$.

\[
\boxed{\int_C \frac{z}{z^2+4}\,dz = 2\pi i}
\]

%-------------------------
\item \textbf{Solution}

Let $z=e^{it}$, $dz=i e^{it}dt$, $0\le t\le2\pi$.

\[
\Re(z)=\cos t
\]

\[
\oint_{|z|=1}\Re(z)\,dz
= i\int_0^{2\pi}\cos t(\cos t+i\sin t)\,dt
\]

Both integrals vanish over $[0,2\pi]$.

\[
\boxed{0}
\]

\item \textbf{Solution}

\[
2z^2-5z+2=(2z-1)(z-2)
\]

Poles: $z=\frac12$ (inside), $z=2$ (outside).

\[
\text{Res}_{z=\frac12}
=\lim_{z\to\frac12}\frac{z-\frac12}{(2z-1)(z-2)}
=-\frac13
\]

\[
\boxed{\oint_{|z|=1}\frac{1}{2z^2-5z+2}\,dz
=-\frac{2\pi i}{3}}
\]
